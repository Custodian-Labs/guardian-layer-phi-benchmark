Does the surrogate stay detectable?

The cleanest test isolates only the spans Custodian actually transformed and asks: does the detector still find the surrogate? Under overlap matching — the detector flags the surrogate, allowing for the boundary jitter a length change introduces — recall retention is 93–100% (97–100% for all but the weakest detector).

SystemExact boundaryOverlap (finds surrogate)

Recall on transformed-only spans, transformed ÷ original. The gap between the two columns is pure boundary noise: surrogates differ in length (“Anna S.” → “Maria S.”), so a found entity with a one-character boundary shift counts as a miss under exact matching but a hit under overlap.

Is the change significant? Pooling all 11 detectors over 56,174 masked spans, recall moves 76.2% → 75.0% (−1.2 pts, 95% CI [−1.5, −1.0]). An equivalence test (TOST) confirms this is statistically equivalent to zero within ±2 points (p ≈ 1×10⁻⁸). With 56k paired observations even a 1-point shift trips a plain significance test, so we report equivalence — the effect is provably too small to matter, not merely "not significant."

Correct vs missed — where the gap comes from

39,550 masked spans are detected in both conditions; 3,261 are found originally but missed after transform. 51% of those are the same length as the original — so it is not a boundary effect. The cause is surrogate quality, not detector failure.

What it means

  • ADetectability survives. On the spans Custodian transforms, on the masked spans detector recall is statistically equivalent before vs after (±2 pts, TOST p≈1e-8) — found 93–100% of the time; the small residual gap is surrogate-quality noise, not PHI being hidden.
  • BRanking is intact. Gemma 4 31B leads and the out-of-domain OBI model trails in both conditions — transform doesn't distort cross-model comparison.
  • CLeakage barely moves (+1.7 to +4.1 pts). Surrogates aren't systematically easier to miss than the PHI they replace.
  • DASQ-PHI is the stress case. Its sparse, short queries amplify any single swap — yet the strongest models still hold above 0.55 F1 there.

Coverage & masking scope

Masking scope. The share of each benchmark's gold PHI the transform actually altered tracks how closely its annotation matches Guardian Layer's proprietary sense of sensitive content: 80.9% on ASQ-PHI and 48.3% on MEDDOCAN (genuine clinical PHI), lower on general-purpose NER/PII whose labels (encyclopedic names, generic locations) fall outside that scope. domain="General" gave the highest coverage in a config sweep. Coverage and utility are reported separately — the 97–100% above is measured only on spans that were in fact transformed.
